Speedtrain generates content with AI, so using it brings obligations under the EU AI Act — some for Enterspeed, some for you. This page sets out how we split that responsibility, what we do on our side, and a practical way to work out what applies to the content you publish.

<Warning>
  This page is our general understanding of the rules, not legal advice. Whether a specific piece of content needs a label is a decision for you and your own legal counsel. We review this page when the regulation or the Commission's guidance changes.
</Warning>

## Who is responsible for what

| | Enterspeed | You |
|---|---|---|
| **EU AI Act role** | Provider of Speedtrain | Deployer — you use Speedtrain under your own authority |
| **GDPR role** | Processor for the data you send through Speedtrain | Controller for that data |
| **Main AI Act obligation** | Machine-readable marking of AI-generated output (Art. 50(2)) | Human-facing disclosure where your content requires it (Art. 50(3) and (4)), and AI literacy for your staff (Art. 4) |

The two sets of obligations are separate. Neither one discharges the other — see [Two common misunderstandings](#two-common-misunderstandings).

## What Enterspeed does

### Marking AI-generated content

As provider, Enterspeed must ensure Speedtrain output is marked in a machine-readable format as artificially generated, as far as technically feasible (Art. 50(2)).

We treat all Speedtrain output as AI-generated by default.

In the Speedtrain app, AI-generated content is clearly identified as such. Machine-readable marking that travels with the output itself depends on the underlying model providers, and support for text watermarking varies between models and model versions. We track the marking each model applies.

### Security and data protection

Enterspeed is ISO 27001 certified and GDPR compliant. Speedtrain runs on the same platform and under the same information security management system as the rest of Enterspeed.

### Our position on risk classification

Speedtrain is designed for enriching, classifying and translating structured business data. Based on that intended purpose, it does not fall under the prohibited practices in Article 5 or the high-risk use cases in Annex III. If you intend to use Speedtrain for a purpose that could fall under Annex III, the Terms of Service require you to tell us first.

## What you are responsible for

As deployer, you decide what Speedtrain output you publish and where. Article 50 gives deployers three separate disclosure obligations. Work through them in order.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Is any of the content image, audio or video that could pass as real?">
    Synthetic images, audio or video that resemble real people, objects, places or events and could be mistaken for authentic are *deep fakes* under the AI Act. They must be disclosed as AI-generated **regardless of subject matter or audience** (Art. 50(4), first subparagraph). There is no editorial-review exemption for this category. Evidently artistic, satirical or fictional work only needs disclosure in a form that doesn't spoil the work.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Is the text published to inform the public on matters of public interest?">
    AI-generated text must be disclosed when it is **published** *and* its purpose is to **inform the public on matters of public interest** (Art. 50(4), second subparagraph). Both conditions must be met. Most Speedtrain use falls outside this — see [Product text](#product-text) below — and there is an exemption for human-reviewed content, described in [The editorial review exemption](#the-editorial-review-exemption).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Does it involve emotion recognition or biometric categorisation?">
    If so, the people exposed to it must be informed (Art. 50(3)). This is unlikely to apply to typical Speedtrain use, but check if you feed biometric-derived data through a task configuration.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Where disclosure is required, it must be clear, given no later than the first time a person encounters the content, and meet accessibility requirements (Art. 50(5)).

## Product text

In our reading, ordinary product and catalogue text — product descriptions, bullet points, category copy, meta descriptions, attribute values, classifications — is not published to inform the public on matters of public interest, and so falls outside Art. 50(4).

What changes that is not the product type but **the claim the text makes**. Text becomes grey the moment it asserts something that must be guaranteed or documented, particularly where it touches health, safety or the environment:

- **Safety.** A work shoe described by fit and colour is product text. Describing its safety properties, protection class or certification touches public safety.
- **Health.** Health or nutrition claims on cosmetics, supplements, food or medical devices.
- **Environment.** Sustainability claims, recycled content, carbon footprint, energy consumption, "climate neutral".
- **Financial products.** Rates, terms and comparisons.
- **Public sector.** Service information published by a public authority to citizens, even when it is structured like catalogue content.

If you are unsure, treat a borderline case as in scope. A single tenant often runs both plain product copy and claim-bearing content through the same Speedtrain setup, so it is worth drawing the line per task configuration.

## The editorial review exemption

The text disclosure obligation does not apply where AI-generated text has undergone **human review or editorial control**, and a natural or legal person holds **editorial responsibility** for its publication. This exemption covers text only — not image, audio or video.

Speedtrain's [review sessions](/speedtrain/key-concepts/review-sessions) support this: every item passes through a session where a person accepts, declines or sends it back, and the session records who approved what.

The exemption is earned by the review actually happening, not by the feature existing. In practice:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What is likely to count">
    - A named person or role holds editorial responsibility for each task configuration — not just "the marketing team".
    - The reviewer has relevant knowledge of the subject matter, and genuinely assesses each item before accepting it.
    - You keep the review session records as evidence that the review took place.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is unlikely to count">
    - Accepting a session in bulk without opening the items.
    - A spelling or grammar pass.
    - Items auto-approved by a [quality gate](/speedtrain/key-concepts/quality-gates) without a person looking at them. The gate is arithmetic, not editorial control.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  If your content makes safety, health or environmental claims, you almost certainly already have someone signing off on them for marketing-law reasons. Make that person the editorial owner for the relevant task configuration.
</Tip>

## Two common misunderstandings

**"Enterspeed marks the output, so we don't need to label it."** Our machine-readable marking is a provider obligation and is invisible to readers. It does not replace the visible disclosure a deployer owes under Art. 50(4) where that applies.

**"We reviewed it, so no marking is needed."** The editorial review exemption removes *your* disclosure obligation for that text. It does not remove any machine-readable marking in the output. Don't strip or degrade provenance markings when you transform or republish Speedtrain content downstream.

## Other obligations that still apply

- **AI literacy (Art. 4).** Deployers must ensure that staff who operate AI systems have sufficient AI literacy. This is separate from Article 50 and applies regardless of what you publish.
- **Marketing and consumer protection law.** Article 50 does not override other transparency and accuracy rules (Art. 50(6)), including the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, national marketing law and the EU rules on environmental claims. A text that doesn't need an AI label is not lawful by default. Guarantees and certifications must be documentable regardless of who — or what — wrote them.

## Data protection

Enterspeed processes the data you send through Speedtrain as your processor, under our data processing agreement.

Which third parties receive that data depends on the model an [AI specialist](/speedtrain/key-concepts/ai-specialists) runs on. Where a task configuration uses your own credentials for a model or tool, the relationship with that provider — including the data processing agreement — is yours.

Speedtrain is built for structured business data such as products and articles. Avoid sending personal data through a task configuration unless you have assessed that it is necessary and lawful.
